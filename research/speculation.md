# WF-4: Lossless speculation schemes for GLM-5.3-Flash's MTP head on a bandwidth-bound APU

Question: which lossless speculation schemes fit GLM-5.3-Flash's MTP head on Strix Halo (about 256 GB/s)?
Method note: Maya facts come from reading the repo at commit 52e9131. Paper numbers come from the cited abstracts or pages, fetched during this research. Anything marked "arithmetic" is my own calculation, not a measurement.

## Answer

1. **Keep a short chained MTP draft (1 to 2, at most 3 drafts) as the base.** Wider or tree drafting does not pay on this hardware. On a memory-bound APU, verify cost grows with the *union of experts* the window touches. For GLM-5.3-Flash (288 routed experts, top-8, 42 MoE layers) each extra window row adds up to about 45% of a lone token's weight traffic (arithmetic below), which is more than the tokens it adds at Maya's measured accept rate. Maya's own measurements agree: a 2-token window costs 1.24x a lone token on 2 GPUs and 1.56x on 1 GPU (`src/core/glm_dflash.cu:1778-1783`, the DFlash path), and the adaptive controller settles on length 1 to 2 on the Halo (`src/core/glm_mtp.cu:441-448`).
2. **Tree drafting (EAGLE-2/3, Medusa-style) is the wrong tool here.** Its benefit shrinks as trees grow, and three 2026 papers (MoE-Spec, EVICT, EcoSpec) show MoE verification cost rising with tree size through expert-union growth. Even the best lossless fix (EcoSpec) gets only 1.15 to 1.36x average speedup at batch 1 on 8xH200. A depth-1 top-2 sibling is the only tree shape worth a later experiment, and I expect it to be marginal (arithmetic below).
3. **The cheapest remaining lossless gain is prompt-lookup / suffix drafting, selected by a cost model.** Maya already has `SuffixDrafter` plus `DraftPolicy` (expected tokens per millisecond, learned online). It is wired into `src/program/generate.cpp` and `src/ds4/ds4_main.cpp`, but **not into the GLM MTP decode loop** (`src/core/glm_mtp.cu` has no reference to it). Maya's own notes report +6 to 11% on edits and unchanged ordinary text on those paths. n-gram drafting literature reports 2 to 4x only on input-grounded tasks (summarization, QA, code edit). Porting it to GLM is the highest-value, lowest-risk next step.
4. **Best combo: MTP chain as the default, plus a lookup window only when a long match exists and its learned acceptance justifies the extra rows.** `DraftPolicy::choose` (`include/strata/spec/draft_policy.hpp`) already implements this decision.
5. **A cheap MoE-aware knob, still lossless:** choose among candidate draft rows by marginal new-expert cost (the EcoSpec idea). With chains of 2 to 3 rows the gain is small; it matters mainly if a lookup window of 6+ rows is verified.
6. **The biggest lever is probably MTP acceptance itself, not scheme choice.** At about 61.6% per-draft acceptance the first draft is worth about 0.62 tokens per round, while the 65 ms verify round is mostly fixed weight traffic. DeepSeek-V3 reports 85 to 90% for its MTP's second token. Whether the gap comes from the GLM head, the 98304-token draft vocabulary, quantization, or sampling is unknown and worth a separate ticket.

## What Maya does today (grounded in the repo)

- **Code:** `src/core/glm_mtp.cu` (one-GPU MTP decode; header comment lines 1-24 describe a round), `include/strata/core/glm_model.hpp` (`kWinMax = 16`), `src/core/glm_window.cu` (verify window), `src/spec/draft_policy.cpp`, `src/spec/suffix_drafter.cpp`, `docs/AMD_MAYA.md` ("Answers and prompts (v1.1.10)"), `docs/SETTINGS.md`.
- **Round structure:** the NextN block drafts d1..dn as a *chain*. Each step's greedy argmax over the draft vocabulary feeds the next step's embedding, on the device when `STRATA_GLM_MTP_CHAIN` is on. The verify window `[y, d1..dn]` runs once through the trunk. A draft stands while it matches the verify pick at its row; the first mismatch becomes the new token, and a full match yields a bonus token. Recurrent state is committed for the kept rows and the NextN cache is refilled with trunk states. Output is exactly the token-at-a-time output for the same sampler draws, so it is lossless for greedy and sampled decoding (header lines 2-17).
- **Where it runs:** one GPU that holds all routed experts. By default that means gfx115x APUs only (Strix and Gorgon Halo). Off with `--parallel` or the DFlash drafter (`mtp_decode_wanted`).
- **Draft length:** hard cap `STRATA_GLM_MTP_DRAFT` (default 3). The adaptive controller `mtp_choose` (from line 441) measures each length's wall time and per-position acceptance and maximizes tokens per second. It starts at `STRATA_GLM_MTP_START=2` and hill-climbs; length 0 (plain token) is only tried when 1 is best. A code comment records that on the Radeon 8065S one draft gives about 22.5 tok/s where two give 21.3 at 57% acceptance. The ticket's "length 1, ~61.6% accept, ~65 ms verify per round" is consistent with this controller landing on 1.
- **Draft vocabulary (98304):** the draft head runs over a draft vocabulary instead of the full 154,880 x 4096 Q6_K head. A full-head step costs about 2.4 ms of about 4 ms per draft step on the 8065S. The vocabulary is the first 98,304 ids (Latin and code block) plus 437 digit-only and control tokens, 64% of the head. A miss costs about 0.9% of tokens per 1% missed, against about 2% of round time saved. If more than 2% of emitted tokens fall outside it (`kOutMax`), drafting falls back to the whole head (`src/core/glm_mtp.cu:104-124`, `566-572`).
- **Existing knobs (env, `STRATA_GLM_*`):** `MTP_DRAFT` (cap), `MTP_ADAPT` (0 = always the cap), `MTP_FIXED=<n>` (forced length for A/B), `MTP_START`, `MTP_ACC_WINDOW` (acceptance decay window, default 64), `MTP_CHAIN`, `MTP_DRAFT_VOCAB=<K>` (0 = whole head), `MTP_DECODE` (0/1), `NO_MTP` / `NO_SPEC`, `MTP_TRACE`, `MTP_KEEP`, `MTP_MISS`, plus the multi-conversation `MAYA_GLM_MULTI_MTP*` set. Generic options in `generate.cpp`: `--spec`, `--mtp-max-t`, `--suffix-draft N` (default 3). The ds4 path has `DS4_DRAFT_COST`. The GLM path has no tree: the draft at each position is a single token.
- **Reported speed on Halo:** CHANGELOG v1.1.10 and `docs/AMD_MAYA.md`: 28.4 to 29.6 tok/s with MTP against 19.7 without on a Gorgon Halo (8065S, Maya-S, short prompts).
- **Suffix drafter (not used by GLM today):** trigram index, 4 ways, matches of 3 to 32 tokens, about 20 bytes per history token (`include/strata/spec/suffix_drafter.hpp`). `DraftPolicy` learns lookup acceptance per match-length bucket and measured round cost per window size, and takes the lookup window only when its E/cost beats the MTP's by a margin. Its own header says an unconditional lookup lost 2 to 8% on ordinary text (`draft_policy.hpp:1-12`). The `--suffix-draft` entry in `docs/SETTINGS.md` reports a Strix Halo whole-file edit 46 to 58 tok/s with DSpark (the DeepSeek path).

## Verify cost vs. draft width on a bandwidth-bound MoE (the core issue)

**Independent-routing model (my arithmetic; GLM-5.3-Flash config from `docs/GLM5-FLASH.md`: 288 experts, top-8, 42 MoE layers, routed share 95%, 18B active).**

- Unique experts per layer for T window rows if every row routes independently: u(T) = 288 * (1 - (1 - 8/288)^T), giving u = 8, 15.8, 23.4, 30.7, 44.6, 57.5 for T = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8.
- Weight traffic is about 9.5B fixed (attention, dense layers, shared expert, head) plus about 1.06B per unique-expert-per-layer. Relative to one lone token: T=2 about 1.45x, T=3 about 1.9x, T=4 about 2.3x, T=8 about 3.4x.
- Real routing is correlated across adjacent tokens, so the true union is smaller than this upper bound. Measured by Maya (DFlash path, not the MTP path): 1.56x for a 2-row window on one GPU and 1.24x on a 2-GPU batched split (`glm_dflash.cu:1781`). The one-GPU figure is above the model's 1.45x; fixed per-window overhead and attention costs are not in the model.
- Expected tokens per round for a chain with constant per-draft acceptance a = 0.616 (generous, since acceptance falls with depth): E = 1.62, 2.00, 2.23 for 1, 2, 3 drafts. Tokens per unit cost (independent model): 1.62/1.45 = 1.11x, 2.00/1.9 = 1.05x, 2.23/2.3 = 0.97x, versus 1.0x for a plain token. With deeper drafts accepting less, length 1 to 2 is the optimum, matching Maya's controller. Per-round overheads (draft step about 4 ms, commit, cache refill) are not included and push toward even shorter windows.
- Tree check: a depth-1 sibling (top-2 branching) is a row with a *different input token*, so its experts barely overlap its sibling's. It costs about +0.4 of a lone token's traffic for perhaps 0.1 to 0.15 extra accepted tokens. I found no measured GLM top-2 coverage figure; this is an estimate.

**Literature on the same effect:**

- MoE-Spec (arXiv 2602.16052, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16052v1): on OLMoE-1B-7B (64 experts, top-8) a 127-token tree activates 54 of 64 experts per layer. EAGLE-3 speedup peaks at tree size about 31 (1.95x) and falls to 1.85x at 255; capping experts per layer recovers 2.1x. It is **not lossless** (it truncates or substitutes experts; OLMoE GSM8K 74 to 66), so it is evidence only, not a candidate scheme. Single request, A100.
- EVICT (arXiv 2605.00342, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00342): lossless tree truncation using drafter signals plus offline-profiled verification cost; up to 2.35x over autoregressive and 1.21x average over EAGLE-3 on SGLang. Abstract-level only.
- EcoSpec (arXiv 2607.12696, https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.12696.md): adds marginal expert-activation cost to draft selection, verifier unchanged (lossless). Batch 1, 8xH200, greedy: Qwen3-235B-A22B 1.36x (EAGLE-3 1.22x), GPT-OSS-120B 1.31x (1.14x), DeepSeek-V3.1 1.15x (its MTP 1.10x). Unique experts per layer per step fell 23.7 to 20.5 on Qwen3-235B. It names the effect "expert scattering": high-confidence draft nodes that route to disjoint experts. Speedups fall with batch size (EAGLE-3 about 1.0x at batch 4). Datacenter GPUs, not an APU.
- MoESD (arXiv 2505.19645, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19645): speculative decoding on MoE is weak at small batch because the expert union inflates verification, and helps most at medium batch (up to 2.29x on Qwen2-57B-A14B). Batch 1 on an APU is the weakest regime.
- "Why Speculative Decoding Works Better Than Predicted on Sparse MoE Models" (NeurIPS 2026 poster, https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/150811): across 10 sparse MoEs (30B to 1T, 8 to 512 experts), routing overlap across draft tokens shrinks the unique-expert count versus an independence model (max-entropy fit, under 4% error), and recent fine-grained models show *higher* speedup at low batch. This matters for GLM-5.3-Flash (fine-grained: 288 experts, top-8): the independent model above is an upper bound on verify cost. **Measure the real union on GLM** before fixing a width policy.

## Scheme-by-scheme survey

| Scheme | Reported results | Fit for this target |
|---|---|---|
| DeepSeek-V3-style MTP, chained (Maya today) | DeepSeek-V3 section 5.4.3: second-token acceptance 85 to 90%, 1.8x TPS (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437v2). The head is trained one token ahead; deeper chain steps reuse it recursively. | Best baseline: no extra big weights read, lossless via exact verify. |
| EAGLE-2 (dynamic tree) | 3.05 to 4.26x on dense models, 20 to 40% above EAGLE-1 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16858) | Tree gains assume cheap verify. MoE trees activate the expert union. Poor fit at batch 1 on an APU. |
| EAGLE-3 | up to 6.5x, about 1.4x over EAGLE-2; SGLang throughput 1.38x at batch 64 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01840) | Needs a trained drafter for GLM-5.3-Flash (none known); same verify-cost issue. |
| Medusa (heads + tree) | Medusa-1 over 2.2x, Medusa-2 2.3 to 3.6x (https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10774) | Needs trained heads; same tree cost. |
| Hardware-aware trees (Sequoia) | up to 4.04x Llama2-7B on A100; picks tree size per hardware (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12374) | The idea applies: size the window from measured cost. Maya's `DraftPolicy` already does this. |
| Prompt lookup / n-gram | 2.4x average on summarization and context-QA, Mistral-7B, A100, greedy; never worse than greedy in the author's runs (https://github.com/apoorvumang/prompt-lookup-decoding). Author's own small runs on a dense 7B. | No drafting cost. Verify rows still cost expert unions, which is why Maya gates it by learned acceptance. Strong on code edits and quoted text. |
| Lookahead decoding (Jacobi + n-gram pool) | up to 1.8x MT-bench, up to 4x multi-GPU code completion; "exact" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02057) | Trades extra per-step compute for fewer steps; its extra rows hit the same expert-union cost. Not recommended. |
| SGLang MTP/EAGLE for GLM | GLM-4.6 example uses steps 3, topk 1, 4 draft tokens; overlap scheduler supports topk 1 only (https://docs.sglang.io/docs/basic_usage/glm45, https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/speculative_decoding.html). The GLM page was read via search summary only. | A mainstream engine also runs GLM MTP as a short chain (topk 1, about 3 steps), consistent with Maya. No accept-length figures given. |

All of these keep the target distribution when run with standard speculative-sampling acceptance (Leviathan et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192; Chen et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318; both cited from memory, not re-fetched). EAGLE-2's abstract states losslessness. Maya's version is exact per sampler draw (counter + row), per the `glm_mtp.cu` header.

## Suggested follow-ups

1. **Port `SuffixDrafter` + `DraftPolicy` into `decode_mtp`.** Rows beyond the MTP chain would come from a lookup match chosen by E/cost. `DraftPolicy` already has `choose/observe`.
2. **Instrument the real expert union on GLM:** per-layer unique experts for T = 2..8 rows on real text, and measured verify ms per T on the Halo. This replaces the arithmetic above and settles how long a lookup window can be.
3. **Investigate the 61.6% acceptance:** compare the draft-vocabulary head against the full head (`STRATA_GLM_MTP_DRAFT_VOCAB=0`) on acceptance and round time, and check the Q8_0 draft projections against the DeepSeek-V3 85 to 90% reference.
4. **Only if 1 to 3 leave headroom:** a depth-1 top-2 sibling experiment, and EcoSpec-style expert-reuse-aware choice of lookup rows.

## Limits of this research

- DeepSeek-V3, EAGLE-2/3, Medusa, Sequoia, Lookahead and PLD numbers come from abstracts, a section quote or READMEs, and the dense-model numbers do not transfer to a bandwidth-bound MoE.
- EVICT and EcoSpec are 2026 preprints read at abstract or summary level; EVICT per-model numbers were not retrieved. The NeurIPS page gave an abstract without speedup multipliers.
- The 61.6% accept, 65 ms verify and "length 1" figures are as supplied in the ticket. The repo confirms the mechanism, but I did not run it. No measurement of GLM's real expert union exists in the repo.
